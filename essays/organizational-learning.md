# When one person learns, how can the organization learn too?

[Home](../README.en.md) · [Full paper](../paper/README.md) · [中文](organizational-learning.zh-CN.md)

> Reading guide | Editorial adaptation of the source paper, pp. 21–24, 27–30. This is not new empirical evidence. The Chinese original governs interpretation.


Between one person completing a task and an organization possessing that capability lies a real handover. Uploading files, finishing training or creating an agent does not by itself close the gap.

The paper names this path P2C2L: personal selection and authorization; source and permission governance; applicable work; independent use and acceptance; then formalization after sustained reuse. Knowledge transfer becomes a testable work process.

VKM uses AI to lower the cost of expressing and organizing experience. Human judgment retains truth, permissions and publication. FusionLoop supplies real work in which the method meets exceptions, failures, repairs and another acceptance decision.

Start with three questions: **Can a second person use it independently? Can someone take over exceptions? Does the next cycle improve net results?** Observe time to independent handover and who maintains the capability.

This explains why incentives should recognize reuse, development and succession. Rewarding only today's delivery can hide work that enables the next person. Recognition still requires actual adoption and outcomes, without counting one result twice under reuse and assets.

The method remains unvalidated. If the second person still requires author rescue, review/maintenance costs dominate, or employees distrust knowledge-use boundaries, organizational learning has not yet been established.
