## Six responsibility roles

These are responsibilities to cover, not a requirement to create six jobs or appoint six people. Combining roles must preserve necessary independence in professional judgment and acceptance.

| Role | Responsibility | Limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business Sponsor | Value, priorities, resources, risk; appoint, replace and evaluate the Owner | Owns delegation and major capacity arbitration |
| Loop Owner | End-to-end integration, orchestration, integration choices, node acceptance and escalation | Highest work-orchestration authority within Sponsor delegation and allocated capacity |
| Line Manager | Formal home, workload, professional output, development, succession and asset supply | Retains formal people-management responsibility |
| Gatekeeper | Legal, finance, architecture, security, quality and other professional judgments, sign-off and vetoes | Independence cannot be overridden by orchestration authority |
| HR / Human-Agent Organization Designer | Institutional design for organization, roles, capacity, talent, performance, incentives and rights | Rules, calibration, fairness, appeals and audit |
| Technology & Risk Governor | Platforms, identity, permissions, data, security, audit, reliability and rollback | Does not replace business acceptance or formal personnel responsibility |

## Three lines of authority

The **outcome line** connects Sponsor and Owner. The **supply line** gives Line Managers responsibility for people and capabilities. The **governance line** preserves professional and institutional boundaries through Gatekeepers, HR and technology/risk governance.

An Owner may integrate work within delegated authority and allocated capacity, but cannot unilaterally change formal reporting, pay grade, employment conditions or formal performance, or bypass non-delegable sign-off. A single Sponsor resolves priorities within their scope. Existing operating governance arbitrates major cross-Sponsor resource conflicts without replacing individual outcome accountability.

Line Managers retain formal evaluation of ordinary members, using Owner evidence of results/collaboration and Gatekeeper evidence of quality/risk. Sponsors evaluate Owners. HR governs rules, calibration, fairness and appeals.

## Three kinds of compilation

| Kind | Translation | Main participants |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business | Intent → accepted outcomes, triggers and scope | Sponsor and Loop Owner |
| Work/technical | Outcome requirements → runnable tasks, context, tools and connections | FDE, product, process, data and IT roles |
| Organizational/institutional | Work changes → roles, capacity, talent, evaluation, incentives and rights | CHRO, HR CoE and HRBP roles |

FDE is one possible participant in work/technical compilation. The three kinds iterate; they are neither three mandatory new jobs nor a linear approval chain.

## Six interfaces to formalize gradually

1. Work classification: auditable, reversible execution versus human intent, commitments, consequential exceptions and acceptance.
2. Responsibility appointments: Sponsor, Owner, capability suppliers and Gatekeepers.
3. Capacity arbitration: explicit allocations and conflict resolution.
4. Role evolution: sustained changes in results, judgments, authority and capabilities.
5. Evaluation/incentives: four contribution types, differentiated timing, evidence and appeals.
6. Employee rights: minimum necessary use, authorization, purpose limits, notice, access, correction and appeal.

The sequence is **operate—collect evidence—calibrate—formalize**, not completing every institutional document before a bounded trial. Agents must not automatically decide performance, promotion, pay, discipline or layoffs.
